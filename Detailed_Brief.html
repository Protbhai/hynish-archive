The Hynish story
from public records

Panchsheel Hynish, Plot GH-08A, Sector-1, Greater Noida (West) · Public records to 4 November 2026

What this is. A neutral account of the society built only from public records: official letters and plans filed with UP RERA, court orders and case status, association notices published online, news reports and property listings. Private messages and documents carrying buyers' personal details are excluded. Official records that reached residents through WhatsApp (court judgments, Authority orders and minutes, Deputy Registrar and election-officer letters, the Deed of Declaration and NPCL notices) are included; those naming private residents are cited, not reproduced. Where sources disagree, both are given. Gaps are listed, not filled. This is not legal advice.

1. The story in six chapters

1. The land (2010-2012)

In March 2010 the Greater Noida Authority allotted a 204,000 sq m group-housing plot, GH-08 in Sector-1, to a consortium led by Supertech with Panchsheel Buildtech as a member. A 30,000 sq m sub-plot, GH-08A, was leased to Panchsheel the same year. Within a year, court rulings on land acquisition in Greater Noida West halted building across the area until 2012. [S73] [S74] [S65] [S67]

2. Building twelve towers (2014-2021)

A revised plan approved in May 2014 kept seven towers of stilt + 14 floors (T1-T7) and added five taller ones: T8-T9 at stilt + 24, T10-T11 at stilt + 20 and T12 at stilt + 21, for 1,312 homes. The later towers were registered with UP RERA in three phases. Tower-10 received its completion certificate in October 2018; by 2019 ten towers were occupied, and the last phase was recorded complete in January 2021. [S75] [S17] [S58] [S20] [S64]

3. Residents organise (2023-2024)

Residents registered an apartment owners' association, reported in February 2023 with a nine-member board under registration GBN/10037/2022-2023. In March 2024 an election under an administration-appointed officer chose a new board, which said its first goal was taking over the society from the builder. [S40] [S01] [S41] [S02]

4. Handover and everyday services (2024)

Through 2024 the AOA held meetings on handover, an MOU with the builder and NPCL multipoint electricity. In May the Authority directed the builder to hand over maintenance and the electricity connection by 15 July; in July the MOU was still "in progress". The AOA took over maintenance on 7 September 2024, by its own records, but the electricity connection stayed in the builder's name. The same year saw water shortages, power-cut protests and complaints about lifts, the pool and maintenance charges, with the AOA, the MP and authorities approached. [S03] [S04] [S05] [S81] [S82] [S89] [S47] [S21] [S49] [S42]

5. Elections in dispute (2025-2026)

The board elected in March 2025 was challenged. Deputy Registrar orders, SDM decisions and two High Court judgments followed, with the courts sending questions back to the administrators. In September 2026 the SDM reportedly upheld the 2025 election; a new petition was registered in the High Court in November 2026 and was pending. [S44] [S06] [S07] [S13] [S09]

6. Where things stand (2026)

Open questions in late 2026 include the AOA election process; NPCL's mandatory multipoint conversion, which its October notice says about 200 residents opposed; registry for Tower-11 and 12 owners, where the Authority gave the builder until about October 2026 to obtain completion and execute sub-leases; a tax-tribunal order on passing benefits to Tower-10 buyers; and resident demands for public accounts. Each is tracked here with what the public record shows and what it does not. [S88] [S79] [S56] [S39] [S35] [S70]

F01. The May deadline was repeatedly oversimplified

Elections · Document-backed

The operative paragraph 16 has two stages: Prescribed Authority decides within one month of certified-copy production; elections follow within one month of that decision. It is not a standalone deadline of 13 June. Actual service and September decision are necessary to evaluate delay. If the reported 30 September decision is confirmed and paragraph 16 applies without modification, 30 October is a date to examine, not an independently established breach.

Elections · Document-backed

20 May correction changes 19 October to 9 October 2025; 27 February to 7 February 2026; and 22 March 2026 to 22 March 2025. Original five-page versions and press reports can carry the old dates. The combined order is the better working reference. Paragraph 10 also prints 17195 rather than 17915; the correction order does not address this, so it is flagged rather than silently changed.
