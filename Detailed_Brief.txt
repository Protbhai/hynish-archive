The Hynish storyfrom public recordsPanchsheel Hynish, Plot GH-08A, Sector-1, Greater Noida (West) · Public records to 4 November 2026
What this is. A neutral account of the society built only from public records: official letters and plans filed with UP RERA, court orders and case status, association notices published online, news reports and property listings. Private messages and documents carrying buyers' personal details are excluded. Official records that reached residents through WhatsApp (court judgments, Authority orders and minutes, Deputy Registrar and election-officer letters, the Deed of Declaration and NPCL notices) are included; those naming private residents are cited, not reproduced. Where sources disagree, both are given. Gaps are listed, not filled. This is not legal advice.
1. The story in six chapters
1. The land (2010-2012)In March 2010 the Greater Noida Authority allotted a 204,000 sq m group-housing plot, GH-08 in Sector-1, to a consortium led by Supertech with Panchsheel Buildtech as a member. A 30,000 sq m sub-plot, GH-08A, was leased to Panchsheel the same year. Within a year, court rulings on land acquisition in Greater Noida West halted building across the area until 2012. [S73] [S74] [S65] [S67]
2. Building twelve towers (2014-2021)A revised plan approved in May 2014 kept seven towers of stilt + 14 floors (T1-T7) and added five taller ones: T8-T9 at stilt + 24, T10-T11 at stilt + 20 and T12 at stilt + 21, for 1,312 homes. The later towers were registered with UP RERA in three phases. Tower-10 received its completion certificate in October 2018; by 2019 ten towers were occupied, and the last phase was recorded complete in January 2021. [S75] [S17] [S58] [S20] [S64]
3. Residents organise (2023-2024)Residents registered an apartment owners' association, reported in February 2023 with a nine-member board under registration GBN/10037/2022-2023. In March 2024 an election under an administration-appointed officer chose a new board, which said its first goal was taking over the society from the builder. [S40] [S01] [S41] [S02]
4. Handover and everyday services (2024)Through 2024 the AOA held meetings on handover, an MOU with the builder and NPCL multipoint electricity. In May the Authority directed the builder to hand over maintenance and the electricity connection by 15 July; in July the MOU was still "in progress". The AOA took over maintenance on 7 September 2024, by its own records, but the electricity connection stayed in the builder's name. The same year saw water shortages, power-cut protests and complaints about lifts, the pool and maintenance charges, with the AOA, the MP and authorities approached. [S03] [S04] [S05] [S81] [S82] [S89] [S47] [S21] [S49] [S42]
5. Elections in dispute (2025-2026)The board elected in March 2025 was challenged. Deputy Registrar orders, SDM decisions and two High Court judgments followed, with the courts sending questions back to the administrators. In September 2026 the SDM reportedly upheld the 2025 election; a new petition was registered in the High Court in November 2026 and was pending. [S44] [S06] [S07] [S13] [S09]
6. Where things stand (2026)Open questions in late 2026 include the AOA election process; NPCL's mandatory multipoint conversion, which its October notice says about 200 residents opposed; registry for Tower-11 and 12 owners, where the Authority gave the builder until about October 2026 to obtain completion and execute sub-leases; a tax-tribunal order on passing benefits to Tower-10 buyers; and resident demands for public accounts. Each is tracked here with what the public record shows and what it does not. [S88] [S79] [S56] [S39] [S35] [S70]

4. What the records show
F01. The May deadline was repeatedly oversimplifiedElections · Document-backedThe operative paragraph 16 has two stages: Prescribed Authority decides within one month of certified-copy production; elections follow within one month of that decision. It is not a standalone deadline of 13 June. Actual service and September decision are necessary to evaluate delay. If the reported 30 September decision is confirmed and paragraph 16 applies without modification, 30 October is a date to examine, not an independently established breach.[S07] [S13] [S09]
F02. Three corrected dates materially affect the chronologyElections · Document-backed20 May correction changes 19 October to 9 October 2025; 27 February to 7 February 2026; and 22 March 2026 to 22 March 2025. Original five-page versions and press reports can carry the old dates. The combined order is the better working reference. Paragraph 10 also prints 17195 rather than 17915; the correction order does not address this, so it is flagged rather than silently changed.[S07]
F03. Some delays predate the current writElections · Document-backedThe High Court itself records that elections notified on 21 February 2026 were stopped by an order of 23 February. That weakens any claim that every delay arose solely from AOA refusing to hold an election. It does not settle whether the October challenge was necessary, proportionate or resident-approved.[S07] [S11] [S09]
F04. No public account of AOA legal spendingGovernance · Not publicResidents publicly demanded accounts in October 2026 and alleged large collections in February 2026; the AOA called the allegations baseless and earlier defended its handling of the water crisis. No audited accounts or case-wise legal-expense statement is public, so neither the allegations nor the rebuttal can be checked from public material.[S14] [S34] [S23]

Why 2024 notices and later records name different presidentsRegistration · Explained by public reportingReporting on the 16 March 2024 election names Anjali Negi as president, matching the 2024 notices. The High Court judgment identifies Vinod Negi as president elected on 22 March 2025, matching later records. These are two different election results, not an inconsistency. F06. A parking-right promise survives a zero-price labelParking · Document-backedOne allotment exemplar itemises covered parking-1 as FOC, while its general clause 30 describes paid, first-come parking and a separate agreement. The separate sublease exemplar grants use of one covered parking in its schedule. These show specific promises need examination even where a separate parking price is zero. They do not establish that every flat has identical terms or decide enforceability.[S18] [S19]
F07. The sublease expressly attempts to exclude parking from handoverParking · Document-backed / legal effect unresolvedClause 30 says common services transfer to the association while parking and certain other facilities will not. The same deed contains a specific one-covered-space usage right and a clause requiring compliance with the Apartment Act/rules. This could help explain a services-versus-parking handover split, but it does not prove the clause is legally effective or the actual MOU adopted it. Obtain the signed MOU, declaration and parking inventory.[S18] [S04] [S05] [S31]
F08. The 180-space gap is not the same as a 180-space regulatory deficitParking · Plan-backedThe drawing records 1,312 homes and 1,132 proposed car spaces, a difference of 180. But it records minimum required car spaces as 1,003, so proposed inventory is 129 above that drawing minimum. These are different comparisons. Actual usable bays, approved later revisions and the total contractual promises still need audit. The sheet alone neither proves compliance today nor identifies owners without rights.[S17]

F22. The Tower-12 order was a direction to decide, not a registry orderRelated cases · Document-backedThe High Court's 9 April 2026 order, quoted in the Authority's 15 July order, asked the Authority to decide a representation within three months and expressed no view on the merits. Reports of "registry within 90 days" overstated it. F23. NPCL's 2026 case for conversion rests on defaults, not consentElectricity · Document-backedThe 2020 notice used the consent route: multipoint unless a majority kept single point. Both 2026 notices are addressed to the builder, which still held the connection, and the AOA jointly; the notices do not establish which party defaulted, and the default letters themselves are not in hand.[S86] [S87] [S88] [S81]

Method, balance and limitsPublic records only: private messages, members-only AOA minutes and accounts, user-supplied images and documents carrying buyers' personal details are excluded.This is a dated archive, not a live status service or a complete court file.
